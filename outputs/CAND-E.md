**1. [unverified_scope]** target: _Two other teams adopted the library into their own ML pipelines_

A strong answer also reveals whether the candidate actually worked through integration challenges with them or merely observed from a distance.

**2. [depth_probe]** target: _Self-calibrating threshold requires zero labeled data_
   - Q: Walk me through how your self-calibrating threshold actually determines what adjustment to make when it encounters a new data point. Specifically, what signal tells it whether to move the threshold up or down if you have no labels to compare against?
   - A strong answer shows: Genuine understanding of the mechanism: the candidate must explain a concrete, unlabeled signal (e.g., distribution shift detection, confidence entropy, input similarity clustering, or another principled unsupervised proxy) that drives threshold movement. A weak answer reveals outsourced knowledge—vague claims about 'self-calibration' without explaining what unlabeled feature actually guides the adjustment, or admission that some implicit labeling or validation set is actually required.

**3. [high_polish_low_artifact]** target: _Disagreement-based routing is non-obvious and explicitly better than confidence thresholds_
   - Q: Walk me through a concrete example where two models disagree on a prediction, but both have high confidence. How would disagreement-based routing handle that case differently than a confidence threshold approach, and what would you expect to see in the outcomes?
   - A strong answer shows: The candidate understands that confidence and disagreement are orthogonal signals—high confidence from both models doesn't mean they're correct (both can be confidently wrong together). A strong answer reveals they can articulate why routing based on *when models diverge* catches systematic failures that uniform confidence thresholds miss, and can reason through what happens when you send such cases to a different handler (expert, human, ensemble) versus a single model.

**4. [depth_probe]** target: _80% cost reduction at parity accuracy on 3,000 sampled support tickets_
   - Q: Walk me through how you determined which 3,000 tickets to sample and why that sampling method was appropriate for validating the 80% cost reduction claim.
   - A strong answer shows: Whether the candidate understands sampling bias, representativeness, and the statistical rigor needed to support a cost/accuracy trade-off claim. A strong answer reveals knowledge of stratification decisions (ticket type, complexity, channel), sample size justification, and why the sample composition matters for generalizing findings. Weak answers (random selection without rationale, or vague 'representative sample' language) suggest outsourced work or surface-level familiarity.

**5. [depth_probe]** target: _Audit notebook provides transparency and compensates for correlated-error blind spot_
   - Q: Walk me through a concrete example where correlated errors would have gone undetected without the audit notebook. What specific pattern would you be looking for in the notebook entries, and how would that pattern differ from what you'd see if errors were independent?
   - A strong answer shows: Genuine understanding of (1) what correlated errors are and how they differ mechanically from independent errors, (2) how the notebook's structure or logging actually makes correlated patterns visible, (3) a realistic failure mode where this matters, and (4) the specific audit trail signals that would catch what traditional QA misses. Weak answers will be vague about the mechanism or confuse correlated errors with systematic bias.
